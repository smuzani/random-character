# Reviewing generated dialogue

Use these evals after changes to prompts, models, or generation settings that can affect production writing.

The first [eval](blood-and-thunder.md) tests Gemini’s Blood and Thunder tone across several setups. It includes passing and failing examples.

[The Price of the Living](../samples/the-price-of-the-living.fountain) is a known passing reference as written. The other sample scripts remain works in progress.

## What we are looking for

Blood and Thunder gives actors fierce, extravagant dialogue driven by what their characters want. Each character still has a distinct voice.

Screenplay action makes the shot clear at a glance. CAPS direct attention. Concrete gestures, movement, and sounds help us follow the encounter.

Read both successful and failed passages to calibrate your judgment. Correct caps can coexist with slow, abstract narration. Brief action can omit a reaction we need.

## How to review

1. Generate each setup with the selected tone.
2. Read each scene once at screenplay speed.
3. Record a verdict for each criterion in the eval.
4. Quote passages that support each verdict.
5. Record strengths and failures, including mixed results.

For the Haven setup, supply only the Fountain metadata and location. Keep the reference scene body available to the reviewer.

The review criteria apply across setups. Different staging, imagery, and tactics can earn a pass.

## Comparing a change

Use the same model, setups, and generation settings for both revisions. Hide revision labels during the first read.

Record the exact model ID, revisions, settings, outputs, errors, and verdicts. Generate multiple outputs per setup to expose variation.

Look for improvement on the targeted criterion and regressions on the others. A tie leaves the intended improvement unproven.

These files define a manual review draft. A runner can use these criteria after review. Production format and length checks also apply to generated outputs.

## Calibrating the rubric

Before using a revised rubric to compare generations, apply it directly to the full authored screenplay. Also apply each relevant criterion to the documented failing passages. For a short excerpt, judge only what the excerpt shows.

The original *The Price of the Living* passed all four criteria in manual review. Nick challenges Huang to name the captives’ birthdays. Huang turns candles into an accusation of misplaced priorities, and Nick turns them back into a reminder of the captives’ humanity. The imagery carries a live argument. Arif’s plain accusation and the Guard’s interrupted warning give the exchange different voices and rhythms. The cough from the truck redirects Nick to a patient. Lila stepping between the men interrupts their quarrel. At the reveal, ELEVEN FACES, KNOCK and GROWL guide attention from the count to the space beneath the truck.

The action criterion also catches the known miss “Steel screams like a dying god.” The opening words supply the sound, but the comparison slows the read. By contrast, PROUD FATHER in the authored opening gives Huang a recognizable bearing in two words. Judge what the image communicates on first reading.
